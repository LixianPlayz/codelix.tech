01

What a business email is

A business email uses your own domain name instead of a free provider. It makes your website, brand, and customer support look more legitimate.

contact @ yourbusiness.com

Good examples

contact@yourbusiness.com

support@yourbusiness.com

hello@yourbusiness.com

Weaker examples

yourbusiness@gmail.com

businesshelp123@yahoo.com

officialstore2025@outlook.com

02

How to create one

1

Buy a domain

Choose a domain that matches your brand, like yourbusiness.com.

2

Pick an email host

Use Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho Mail, Namecheap, or your website host.

3

Create inboxes

Start with contact@, support@, billing@, or hello@ depending on your site.

4

Connect DNS records

Add the records your email provider gives you so your domain knows where email should go.

5

Test everything

Send test emails in both directions before putting the address on your website.

03

How to place it on your website

The easiest way is to use a clickable mail link. This opens the visitor's email app with your address already filled in.

HTML 
<a href="mailto:contact@yourbusiness.com">
  contact@yourbusiness.com
</a>

Best places to show it

Footer, contact page, support page, quote request page, and legal pages.

Best button text

Email Support, Contact Sales, Request a Quote, or Send Us a Message.

What to avoid

Do not paste your personal email everywhere unless you are fine with spam.

04

How to reduce spam

Bots scan websites for email addresses. A contact form or hidden reveal button can reduce junk mail without making the site harder to use.

Email is hidden until someone clicks.

Safer Display 
<button data-reveal-email>Reveal Email</button>
<p class="email-output"></p>
05

DNS records you should know

MX

Tells the domain where incoming mail should be delivered.

SPF

Lists which servers are allowed to send mail for your domain.

DKIM

Adds a digital signature so receiving inboxes can verify your emails.

DMARC

Gives instructions for handling emails that fail SPF or DKIM checks.
